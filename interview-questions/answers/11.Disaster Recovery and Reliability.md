

# 11. Disaster Recovery and Reliability

## 1. What are the different disaster recovery strategies in AWS?

**Answer:**

AWS defines DR strategies based on **cost vs recovery speed trade-off**:

### 1. Backup and Restore (Lowest cost)

* Take backups (S3, EBS snapshots, RDS snapshots)
* Restore in another region when disaster happens

👉 Pros: Cheap
👉 Cons: Slow recovery

---

### 2. Pilot Light

* Keep minimal core system running (like DB replication)
* Start rest of services during disaster

👉 Pros: Faster than backup
👉 Cons: Still some delay

---

### 3. Warm Standby

* Full system running at smaller scale
* Scale up during failure

👉 Pros: Faster recovery
👉 Cons: Medium cost

---

### 4. Multi-Site (Active-Active)

* Full system running in multiple regions
* Traffic distributed

👉 Pros: Near zero downtime
👉 Cons: Very expensive

---

### Critical thinking:

Many assume **multi-site is best** → not always
👉 Overkill for small systems → wastes money

---

## 2. What is the difference between RTO and RPO?

**Answer:**

### RTO (Recovery Time Objective)

* How fast system must be restored after failure

### RPO (Recovery Point Objective)

* How much data loss is acceptable

---

### Simple example:

* RTO = 1 hour → system must be back in 1 hour
* RPO = 10 minutes → max data loss = 10 min

---

### Key insight:

* Low RTO + Low RPO = **high cost**

👉 Trade-off is always required

---

### Common mistake:

People confuse:

* RTO = time to recover
* RPO = data loss window

---

## 3. How do you design backup and recovery solutions?

**Answer:**

### Step-by-step approach:

### 1. Define requirements

* Business needs:

  * RTO
  * RPO

👉 Without this → design is guesswork

---

### 2. Choose backup strategy

* EBS snapshots
* RDS automated backups
* S3 versioning + replication

---

### 3. Store backups safely

* Cross-region replication
* Multi-AZ storage

---

### 4. Automate backups

* Use lifecycle policies
* Scheduled snapshots

---

### 5. Test recovery

* Regular restore testing

👉 Reality:
Most teams **never test backups** → biggest risk

---

### 6. Secure backups

* Encryption
* IAM access control

---

### Challenge your assumption:

Backup ≠ Recovery
👉 If restore is slow or broken → backup is useless

---

## 4. How do you design high availability across multiple AZs?

**Answer:**

High Availability (HA) means system stays **operational even if one AZ fails**.

---

### Key design principles:

### 1. Use Multi-AZ deployment

* Deploy instances in different AZs

---

### 2. Load Balancer

* Use ALB to distribute traffic

---

### 3. Stateless application design

* Store session in:

  * Redis (ElastiCache)
  * DynamoDB

👉 Why?
So any instance can handle requests

---

### 4. Database HA

* Use RDS Multi-AZ
* Automatic failover

---

### 5. Auto Scaling

* Replace failed instances automatically

---

### 6. Health checks

* Detect failures and reroute traffic

---

### Reality check:

Many say “Multi-AZ = high availability” → incomplete

👉 If app is:

* Stateful
* Not load balanced
* No failover logic

→ It will still fail

---

## Final Interview Insight

Good answer = **trade-offs + reasoning**

* Critical apps → Warm standby / Multi-site
* Normal apps → Pilot light
* Budget apps → Backup & restore

---

## Deeper Thinking (Important)

You should challenge yourself:

👉 “Do we really need 99.999% availability?”

Because:

* Higher availability = exponentially higher cost
* Sometimes business does NOT need it
